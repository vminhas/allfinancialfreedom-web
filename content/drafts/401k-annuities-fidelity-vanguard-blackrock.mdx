For 40 years, the financial industry told American workers that a 401(k) was the answer. Stuff money in, ride the market, retire rich. Pensions were dead. Annuities were boring. The S&P 500 would handle the rest.

In 2025, the three largest names in retirement money quietly changed the script.

BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager with **$13 trillion under management**, rolled out LifePath Paycheck, a target-date fund that converts a portion of your 401(k) balance into a guaranteed lifetime income stream as you approach retirement. Fidelity launched Guaranteed Income Direct, letting plan participants purchase an income annuity straight from their 401(k). Vanguard has partnered with insurers to embed similar options into its plans.

The pitch is identical at all three firms: a 401(k) alone is not enough. You need guaranteed income.

That is not a small concession. It is the largest course correction the retirement industry has made in a generation. And it tells you something important about where the next 20 years are heading.

Here is what is actually happening, why it is happening now, and why the version Wall Street is selling is still a compromise.

---

## What Just Changed

The shift is real, and the numbers behind it are not subtle.

- **BlackRock LifePath Paycheck** launched in April 2024. By the end of its first year, it had reached more than **$26 billion in commitments** across more than a dozen large employer plans, with millions of workers eligible.
- **Fidelity Guaranteed Income Direct** lets participants in supported 401(k) plans use a portion of their balance to buy an immediate income annuity at retirement, with quotes from multiple insurers side by side.
- **State Street, Prudential, TIAA, and Income America** have launched competing in-plan annuity products. The Department of Labor estimates **more than 60 large plan sponsors** have added some form of guaranteed income option since 2022.

This was effectively illegal to do at scale until recently. The **SECURE Act of 2019** created a fiduciary safe harbor that protects employers from being sued for picking the "wrong" annuity provider, as long as they follow a documented process. **SECURE 2.0 in 2022** expanded the framework. Once the legal risk was off the table, the largest plan sponsors moved.

For four decades the industry told workers their accumulation was the goal. Hit a million dollars and you win. Now the same firms are telling those same workers that a pile of money is not the same thing as a paycheck, and you need to convert one into the other.

---

## Why Wall Street Suddenly Cares About Income

This is not generosity. Three forces are converging at the same time, and the industry is responding to the math.

**1. Pension extinction is now total.**
In 1975, roughly **88% of private-sector workers** with a workplace retirement plan had a defined-benefit pension. Today, that number is closer to **15%**, almost all of it in legacy plans that are closed to new hires. An entire generation is retiring without the guaranteed monthly check their parents took for granted.

**2. The 2022 retiree panic was a warning shot.**
In 2022, a 60/40 portfolio lost roughly **17%** for the year, its worst showing since 2008. Retirees who had just stopped working watched their nest eggs shrink while they were drawing down on them. That is sequence of returns risk in real time, and it shook the industry. Surveys from Allianz and Northwestern Mutual have shown for years that more than **60% of Americans fear running out of money more than they fear death**. After 2022, that fear stopped being theoretical.

**3. The asset managers do the math too.**
A 65-year-old who blows through their account at age 78 is no longer a customer. A 65-year-old whose account is structured to produce income for life stays a customer until age 95. BlackRock, Fidelity, and Vanguard are not just protecting retirees. They are protecting their own assets under management.

---

## What This Validates

Strip away the branding and the press releases, and the message from the three biggest names in retirement money is the same one we have been making at AFF for years:

> A market-based account, without a guaranteed income layer, is not a retirement plan. It is a hope.

Every conversation we have with an agent training to serve families starts with the same idea. The 401(k) was sold as a complete solution. It was never one. It is a tax-deferred accumulation account with no built-in protection against market losses in your 60s, no built-in protection against outliving your money, and no built-in income.

For decades, anyone who pointed this out was dismissed as an annuity salesman. In 2025, BlackRock, Fidelity, and Vanguard quietly agreed.

If you are still operating on the assumption that a 401(k) and Social Security are enough, the three companies managing trillions of dollars of other people's retirement money have just told you they are not.

---

## Where the 401(k) Version Falls Short

That said, the in-plan annuities being rolled out are a compromise. They had to be. To fit inside a 401(k) plan, they have to be simple enough for a recordkeeper to administer, cheap enough for an employer to defend, and limited enough that they do not blow up the rest of the plan menu.

Here is what that looks like in practice:

**You do not pick the insurer.**
LifePath Paycheck uses one or two pre-selected carriers chosen by BlackRock. Fidelity's Guaranteed Income Direct does at least show side-by-side quotes, but the carrier menu is curated. You get the carrier the plan picked, not the strongest one for your situation.

**It is bolted onto a target-date fund.**
LifePath Paycheck embeds the annuity inside a target-date fund. That means the underlying investments are still subject to whatever blend of stocks and bonds the fund holds in your age band. There is no zero-loss floor on the non-annuity portion, and your protection only kicks in for the slice that converts.

**There is no living-benefit rider in the plan version.**
Outside of a 401(k), modern fixed indexed annuities and indexed universal life policies offer riders that pay benefits for chronic illness, terminal illness, or long-term care. The in-plan annuities being offered are stripped-down lifetime income contracts. The features that protect a family through a health event are not on the menu.

**You cannot move it.**
Most in-plan annuities are tied to the employer's plan. If you leave the company, the rules around portability are still being written. Outside the plan, you control the contract.

**Fees layer on fees.**
You are paying a target-date fund expense ratio, the annuity's mortality and expense charge, and any administrative fees inside the plan. The same dollars in a standalone product are paying for one contract, not three.

## What This Means for You

If you are 50+ and you have a 401(k) or TSP balance, this is the moment to ask a different question than the one you have been asked your whole career.

The old question was: **how do I grow this faster?**

The new question, the one BlackRock and Fidelity are now asking out loud, is: **how do I turn this into income that cannot run out?**

There are two ways to answer that question.

You can use the in-plan option your employer offers, if they offer one. It is better than nothing. For someone who will never look at their retirement plan in detail, an automatic guaranteed-income conversion inside a target-date fund is a meaningful upgrade over what most workers have today.

Or you can structure it yourself, outside the plan, with a standalone fixed indexed annuity, an indexed universal life policy, or a properly built combination of both. You pick the carrier. You pick the income rider. You pick the living benefits. You pick the chassis. The same dollar of premium does more work because it is not paying for three layers of administration.
